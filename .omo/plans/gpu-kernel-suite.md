# gpu-kernel-suite - Work Plan

## TL;DR (For humans)
<!-- Fill this LAST, after the detailed plan below is written, so it summarizes the REAL plan. -->
<!-- Plain English for a non-engineer: NO file paths, NO todo numbers, NO wave/agent/tool names. -->

**What you'll get:** One unified C++ toolkit suite ("kernscope") for GPU kernels: a static analyzer that reads PTX or AMD assembly and reports occupancy, bank conflicts, divergence, spills, and concrete optimization suggestions; an autotuner that generates and benchmarks kernel variants for GEMM/softmax/layernorm/reduction/attention and remembers the best per GPU; a safety verifier that statically rejects out-of-bounds, racy, and barrier-broken kernels before they run; a capture-and-replay system that records kernels from real apps and rebuilds them as standalone benchmarks; LLVM optimization passes with before/after proof; and a profiler that explains "why is this slow" in compiler terms - all wired into one loop, living on your AMD card locally, with NVIDIA paths compile-proven locally and cloud-validated when you rent GPUs.

**Why this approach:** The six tools share one foundation (GPU architecture database, assembly parsers, occupancy/roofline engines, benchmark and correctness harnesses, report engine), and their real value is the loop they form - profile, capture, analyze, transform, verify, benchmark - which is exactly what recent research (Mneme, Kerncap, R3) validates as the useful artifact; building them as one project also reuses the exact toolchain your previous projects already proved on this machine.

**What it will NOT do:** It will not run NVIDIA GPUs locally (there is none here - NVIDIA paths are compile-tested locally and only run when you rent hardware, never automatically). It will not claim to beat cuBLAS or production stacks. It will not grow frontends, serving endpoints, dataflow simulation, or LLM-driven generation - none of which were asked for.

**Effort:** XL
**Risk:** High - GPU runtime interception and verifier precision on real kernels are the hard parts; mitigated by mock-runtime oracles, a live AMD loop, committed verdict corpora, and patterns proven in your sibling projects.
**Decisions to sanity-check:** merged into ONE project named "kernscope" (MIT license default); LLVM 21 + CUDA 12.6 pins (proven locally, documented migration notes for CUDA 13); capture flipped to CUPTI-primary on NVIDIA with LD_PRELOAD as fallback (research overturned the original LD_PRELOAD-first draft); RDNA3 constants resolved to 1536 VGPRs/SIMD per the ISA doc (conflicting sibling claim documented and overruled).
